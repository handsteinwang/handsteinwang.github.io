Computational Gradient Flows and Optimal Transport Lecture 1

PKU Summer School 2026

Lecturer: Jia-jie Zhu

Lecture Notes — July 20, 2026

Gradient Flows in Euclidean Space

Let $F:\mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}$ be differentiable. The gradient flow of $F$ is the ODE

$$
\dot{x}(t) ;=; -\nabla F\bigl(x(t)\bigr)^{\top}, \qquad x \in \mathbb{R}^d .
$$

Example 1 (Quadratic potential).
Let $F(x) = \tfrac12 \lVert x-m \rVert^2$. Then

$$
\dot{x} = -(x-m),
$$

whose solution is

$$
x(t) = (1-e^{-t})\ m + e^{-t} x(0), \qquad t \ge 0 .
$$

Consequently the energy decays exponentially:

$$
\tfrac12\lVert x(t)-m \rVert^2 ;=; e^{-2t}\ \tfrac12 \lVert x(0)-m \rVert^2 .
$$

Example 2 (Double-well potential).
Let $F(x) = \tfrac14 (x^2-1)^2$, so that $\nabla F(x) = x^3 - x$. The gradient flow reads

$$
\dot{x}(t) = -\bigl(x^3(t) - x(t)\bigr) = -\ x(t)\bigl(x(t)-1\bigr)\bigl(x(t)+1\bigr),
$$

with stable equilibria at $x = \pm 1$ and an unstable equilibrium at $x = 0$.

Convexity in $(\mathbb{R}^d, \lVert\cdot\rVert)$

Definition 3 ($\lambda$-convexity).
A function $F: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}$ is $\lambda$-convex if for all $t \in [0,1]$ and all $x,y \in \mathbb{R}^d$,

$$
F\bigl((1-t)x + ty\bigr) ;\le; (1-t) F(x) + t\ F(y) ;-; \frac{\lambda}{2}\ t(1-t)\ \lVert x-y \rVert^2 .
$$

The parameter $\lambda$ classifies the convexity:

Condition Name
$\lambda = 0$ convex
$\lambda = 0$ with strict inequality strictly convex
$\lambda > 0$ strongly convex
$\lambda < 0$ semi-convex

Differentiable characterizations.

If $F$ is differentiable, then $F$ is $\lambda$-convex if and only if

$$
F(y) ;\ge; F(x) + \langle \nabla F(x),\ y-x \rangle + \frac{\lambda}{2}\lVert y-x \rVert^2 \qquad \forall\ x,y \in \mathbb{R}^d .
$$

If $F$ is twice differentiable, then $F$ is $\lambda$-convex if and only if

$$
\nabla^2 F(x) ;\succeq; \lambda I \qquad \forall\ x \in \mathbb{R}^d .
$$

Gradient Domination (Polyak–Łojasiewicz)

Theorem 4 (Gradient domination / PL inequality).
If $F$ is $\lambda$-convex with $\lambda > 0$, then $F$ satisfies the gradient domination inequality

$$
\frac12 \lVert \nabla F(x) \rVert^2 ;\ge; \lambda\Bigl( F(x) - \inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^d} F \Bigr) \qquad \forall\ x \in \mathbb{R}^d .
$$

Proof. By $\lambda$-convexity, for all $x,y \in \mathbb{R}^d$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(y) &\ge F(x) + \langle \nabla F(x),\ y-x \rangle + \frac{\lambda}{2}\lVert y-x \rVert^2 \\\
&= F(x) + \frac{\lambda}{2}\Bigl\lVert y - x + \tfrac{1}{\lambda}\nabla F(x)\Bigr\rVert^2 - \frac{1}{2\lambda}\lVert \nabla F(x) \rVert^2 \\\
&\ge F(x) - \frac{1}{2\lambda}\lVert \nabla F(x) \rVert^2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

Taking the infimum over $y$ gives

$$
\frac{1}{2\lambda}\lVert \nabla F(x) \rVert^2 ;\ge; F(x) - \inf F . \qquad \blacksquare
$$

Fact 5 (Exponential decay of the energy).
Gradient domination of $F$ implies, along any solution $x(t)$ of $\dot{x}(t) = -\nabla F(x)$,

$$
F\bigl(x(t)\bigr) ;\le; e^{-2\lambda t} F\bigl(x(0)\bigr) + \bigl(1 - e^{-2\lambda t}\bigr)\inf F .
$$

Proof. Differentiating along the trajectory,

$$
\frac{d}{dt} F\bigl(x(t)\bigr) = \langle \nabla F(x),\ \dot{x} \rangle = -\lVert \nabla F(x) \rVert^2 ;\le; -2\lambda\bigl(F(x) - \inf F\bigr).
$$

Grönwall’s inequality then yields

$$
F\bigl(x(t)\bigr) \le e^{-2\lambda t} F\bigl(x(0)\bigr) + \bigl(1-e^{-2\lambda t}\bigr)\inf F . \qquad \blacksquare
$$

Example 6 (Cubic ODE).
Consider the ODE $\dot{x} = -x - x^3$, which is the gradient flow of

$$
F(x) = \tfrac12 x^2 + \tfrac14 x^4 + \mathrm{const}.
$$

Since $\nabla^2 F(x) = 1 + 3x^2 \ge 1$, the function $F$ is $1$-strongly convex, and therefore $F(x(t))$ decays exponentially with rate $2\lambda = 2$ along the ODE trajectory.

Evolutionary Variational Inequality (EVI)

Consider the squared distance to a reference point $m \in \mathbb{R}^d$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dt}\Bigl(\tfrac12\lVert x-m \rVert^2\Bigr)
&= \langle x-m,\ \dot{x} \rangle = -\langle x-m,\ \nabla F(x) \rangle \\\
&\le F(m) - F(x) - \frac{\lambda}{2}\lVert \nabla F(x) \rVert^2 .
\end{aligned}
\tag{$*$}
$$

Special case. Suppose $m \in \arg\min F$. Then $(*)$ reduces to

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\Bigl(\tfrac12\lVert x-m \rVert^2\Bigr) ;\le; -\frac{\lambda}{2}\lVert x-m \rVert^2,
$$

hence

$$
\tfrac12\lVert x(t)-m \rVert^2 ;\le; e^{-\lambda t}\Bigl(\tfrac12\lVert x(0)-m \rVert^2\Bigr),
$$

i.e. $\tfrac12\lVert \cdot - m \rVert^2$ is an exponentially decaying Lyapunov function.

General case. Applying Grönwall’s inequality to $(*)$, for any $m \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tfrac12\lVert x(t)-m \rVert^2
&\le \frac{e^{-\lambda t}}{2}\lVert x_0 - m \rVert^2 + \int_0^t e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\bigl(F(m) - F(x(s))\bigr)\ ds \\\
&\le \frac{e^{-\lambda t}}{2}\lVert x_0 - m \rVert^2 + M_\lambda(t)\ \bigl(F(m) - F(x(t))\bigr),
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
M_\lambda(t) ;=; \int_0^t e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\ ds ;=;
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{1 - e^{-\lambda t}}{\lambda}, & \lambda \neq 0, \\\
t, & \lambda = 0 .
\end{cases}
$$

This is the $\lambda$-evolutionary variational inequality $(\mathrm{EVI}_\lambda)$.

Corollary 7 (Displacement bound from the initial point).
Picking $m = x_0$ in $(\mathrm{EVI}_\lambda)$ gives

$$
\tfrac12\lVert x(t) - x_0 \rVert^2 ;\le; M_\lambda(t)\ \bigl(F(x_0) - F(x(t))\bigr).
$$
